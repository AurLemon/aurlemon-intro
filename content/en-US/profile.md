## Why Junior College

Next, let me answer the question, "How did you end up in junior college?" More precisely, the question should be, "Why did you not go to a regular high school?"

I took the Fuzhou entrance exam in 2021. My score passed the general-high-school cutoff, but not enough for a particularly good school. I also did not seriously research the application process, so I went straight into the five-year vocational path. Looking back, I do not regret this route. It was a detour from the five-year vocational track to the transfer-to-undergraduate path, but it still brought me here. I do not know what my life would look like if I had gone to a regular high school.

As a middle-school student, I had no clear goal and was mostly pushed forward by circumstances. I usually worried about "What if I cannot learn this?" rather than asking "How should I learn?" When I encountered something I did not understand, I rarely tried to break down the problem and look for a method. I mostly went back and forth between anxiety and avoidance. But reducing all of that to "I simply did not work hard enough" would be too easy. I genuinely did not know what to do or whom to ask. I lacked a sufficient frame of reference and an effective feedback loop.

Between 2021 and 2024, I read many study methods and tried to find a ready-made answer in other people's experiences. I consumed countless Xiaohongshu study tips and Bilibili tutorials, but very little of it was genuinely useful. When anxious, I even tried to find certainty in patterns with no basis at all. The problem in that period was not only my grades. I did not know how to face a problem I could not solve yet.

The five-year vocational track itself also caused me a long period of discomfort. My middle school was a high-standard reference frame, surrounded by many excellent people, and words like "technical secondary school" and "junior college" were not exactly compliments there. When they actually appeared on my own school record, I found it absurd for a while.

Back then, I also easily treated small online circles as the whole world. I encountered computers early, built sites, edited videos, made PPTs, and felt proud whenever I knew something others did not. It looks childish now, but it was not completely meaningless. At least computing was one of the few things I was willing to keep investing in, and it helped me settle on a general direction early.

So why did I choose computing in the five-year track? The reason was simple. I did not know much at the time, and "playing with computers, making things, and tinkering with technology" happened to be what felt most familiar. These five years did not give me any particularly legendary answer. They only slowly taught me that anxiety, analysis, and waiting do not make problems disappear. What usually helps is to start with something small and keep adjusting based on the result. Rather than waiting for a definite answer, it is more important to see what I can do right now.
